  Filing: 10-Q, accession 0000006201-26-000065, Note 5, page 16 ($ millions)

3. BHC: FEASIBLE, BUT ONLY AS AN ENTITY-LEVEL MODEL
---------------------------------------------------
The 10-Q (pages 16-22) shows three separate debt pools:

  1. B+L - Bausch + Lomb's own term loan (2,788) and secured notes
     (1,400 + 765). Secured only by B+L's assets (page 20).

  2. 126NumberCo - a BHC subsidiary that owns 211,963,893 B+L shares.
     It borrowed a 2030 term loan B (2,962) and the 10% 2032 notes (6,000).
     That debt has a first lien on the B+L shares AND a pari passu
     first-lien guarantee from the rest of BHC (pages 18, 21).

  3. BHC RemainCo (the rest of BHC) - existing 1L notes (803 + 888),
     14% 2L notes (352) and $4.1B of unsecured notes. These notes are NOT
     guaranteed by 126NumberCo (pages 21-22), so they reach B+L's value only
     through whatever equity is left in 126NumberCo.

Can be modeled from EDGAR data alone:
  - Value B+L from its own 10-K, subtract B+L's debt, and multiply what is
    left by 126NumberCo's ownership share.
  - That stake pays the 126NumberCo debt first.
  - Any shortfall becomes a 1L claim on RemainCo alongside the existing 1L
    notes, followed by 2L, then unsecured.
More involved than the other three companies; the README would list the
simplifications. Doing it last makes sense.
